## Step 0: what this repo is

- **Stack:** pnpm and Turborepo monorepo. Both sites run on Next.js (pages router) but serve plain HTML, so the plain-HTML recipe (Part 13.2) applies. There is no App Router `app/`.
  - `apps/site` is www.capitolreceipts.com: static marketing pages in `apps/site/public/`.
  - `apps/web` is app.capitolreceipts.com and admin.capitolreceipts.com: the app is a single-page app in `apps/web/public/`, and every record page is server-rendered by `apps/web/server/seo.js`.
- **The marketing site is in this repo**, so the full pass applied.
- **Main domain:** `www.capitolreceipts.com`, which matches the Part 2 table.
  - The bare domain and http both 308 to https://www.
  - Record pages on the app host 301 to www.
  - The app host's root is the app itself, set to `noindex,follow`.
  - Admin sends `X-Robots-Tag: noindex, nofollow`.
  - `*.vercel.app` preview hosts send `X-Robots-Tag: noindex`.
- **Repo rules vs the playbook:** there's no `CLAUDE.md`. `README.md` and `docs/OPERATIONS.md` still described the pre-monorepo layout (`site/`, `api/`); I updated them, and nothing conflicts with the playbook. One choice to note: public pages carry `<meta name="robots" content="index,follow,max-image-preview:large">` where the playbook prefers no robots tag. It doesn't block anything and allows large image previews, so I left it.
- **Public pages:**
  - **14 marketing pages:** home, features, receipts, parties, appointees, quiz, how-it-works (the methodology page), newsrooms, pricing, about, support, privacy, terms, delete-account.
  - **73 hub pages:** members, states, bills, votes, rankings and the other lists.
  - **About 160,000 record pages:**
    - people: members, past members, appointees, nominees, judges
    - money and influence: PACs, lobbying clients and firms, companies, outside groups
    - Congress records: bills, votes, committees, caucuses, ethics statements
    - places and races: states, districts, 2026 races
    - trading: stocks, official trades
    - writing: monthly reports, guides
  - **Spanish versions** of members, states, districts, races and their hubs, under `/es/`.
- **Not public:** app screens (app host root, `/go/*`), admin, the `/api/*` endpoints and shared quiz scores (`noindex`).

Sitemap check after the fixes:

- **Files:** the index and all 35 child files parse as valid XML.
- **Addresses:** 175,186, all absolute https addresses on www.
- **Spot check:** 116 sampled addresses (every marketing page plus 3 random ones from each file) all returned 200.
- **Dates:** every record file has dates. The hubs, places and Spanish files don't, which the sitemap rules allow.
- **Duplicates:** the hubs file was still the cached copy with `/` and `/stocks`; it refreshes within a day.

Problem found during the checks, and fixed:

- **Unreadable pages for some readers:** www's cache served brotli-compressed record pages to clients that never asked for compression, so they saw unreadable bytes. `/official-trades/donald-j-trump` was one. Googlebot asks for brotli, so it wasn't affected, but some crawlers and tools were.
- **The fix:** record pages now send `Vary: Accept-Encoding`, so new cache entries are kept separate for clients that do and don't accept compression. Copies cached before the fix clear within a week, or right away if you purge (owner to-do 3).

## Next content opportunities

Each needs the data or expertise behind it, which this app already has for most of them:

1. **Industry pages** ("Who the oil and gas industry funds in Congress"). One page per industry: PAC money by member, stock trades by members in that industry, committee ties. The data is mostly there (industry tags on stocks, PAC giving per member). Intent: research and reporting on industry influence.
2. **2026 election results on each race page.** Who won, the final money raised and outside spending. Intent: "who won [state] [district] 2026" searches peak right now.
3. **"How to look up your representative's stock trades" guide.** A step-by-step guide with real examples and links to the House and Senate disclosure sites. Intent: how-to searches from voters and students.
4. **Notable trades pages.** One page per trade that drew attention: trades made shortly before a related committee action or vote, in neutral wording with the filings linked. Intent: people looking up a trade they read about in the news.
5. **"What is a super PAC" and "How campaign finance works" guides.** These would join the existing STOCK Act and PAC limits guides, with live numbers from the outside-groups data. Intent: explainers.
6. **Spanish versions of the rankings, guides and monthly reports.** Only members, states, districts and races are in Spanish today. Intent: the same searches in Spanish.
7. **Committee-by-industry pages** ("Which committee oversees banking, and who on it trades bank stocks"). Built from the committee jurisdiction and trade data. Intent: conflict-of-interest research.
8. **A "Cite this data" page for journalists.** Stable addresses, the update schedule, suggested citation wording and the corrections process. Intent: reporters looking for a citable source, which also earns links (Part 14.1).
