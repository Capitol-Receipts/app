What you can look up

  • Every member of Congress: donors, trades, votes, bills, former staff who now lobby, and office spending.
  • Stocks: which members bought or sold a company, and when.
  • 2026 races: who ran in all 470 House and Senate races, what they raised, and outside spending.
  • Bills: summaries, how each member voted, and the groups that lobbied on them.
  • PACs and lobbying groups: who gives, who spends, and on what.
  • Appointees: the President’s nominees, their past pay, what they agreed to sell, and their confirmation votes.
  • Past members: everyone who has served since 1789.

How we count

Every member is measured with the same formulas, and every number links to the filing it came from. We don’t give grades. A high number isn’t proof of wrongdoing: trading stock is legal, and a missed vote can mean an illness or a funeral. Read the methods.

Common questions

What is Capitol Receipts?

A free website and app that shows who funds each member of Congress, what stocks they trade, how they vote and who lobbies them. Every number comes from a public filing.

Is it free?

Yes. Every record is free to look up. Pro adds same-day trade alerts, a stock watchlist, the conflict radar and spreadsheet downloads for $5.99 a month or $39.99 a year.

Where does the data come from?

The Federal Election Commission, House and Senate stock trade reports, Voteview roll-call records, the Senate lobbying database and the Office of Government Ethics. How we count lists all nine sources.

How often is it updated?

Every day. New trade reports, campaign filings and votes appear within a day of being posted.

Does it favor one party?

No. Both parties are on every list and every member is measured with the same formulas. We take no money from parties, campaigns, PACs or lobbyists.

How do I find my representative?

Pick your state above, or browse the states. In the app you can type your ZIP code.
